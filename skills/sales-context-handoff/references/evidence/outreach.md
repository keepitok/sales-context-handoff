# Preserve outreach that worked

Modified 2026-11-20 from zarif3624/develop-positioning. This adaptation collects existing messaging evidence; it does not develop new positioning or campaigns.

## Find the sequence

Start from selected closed-won deals, but verify origin. An inbound inquiry, referral or expansion is not evidence that cold outreach succeeded. If no opening message survives, say it was not found.

Capture the exact opening message, meaningful follow-ups and relevant buyer replies in chronological order. Preserve channel, subject when present, sender/recipient roles and dates. Keep line breaks and wording; label redactions and omissions. If the sequence is partial, say which part is absent.

Also collect up to 10 additional examples with a documented reply or meeting outside the closed-deal sample. Count these as outreach examples, not additional won deals. Prefer examples with a substantive response or explicit meeting acceptance; do not promote delivery receipts or opens to successes.

## Outcome distinctions

- reply: a recipient response is present. Describe positive, negative or neutral meaning only when supported.
- meeting: the buyer accepted/booked or attended a meeting; distinguish booked from held in the extract.
- associated_closed_won: linkage to a recorded won opportunity exists; cite the deal outcome as well as the outreach.
- no_reply: a comparison example with no reply visible in the captured scope, not a lost deal or an extra success.
- blank: outcome evidence is unavailable or conflicting.

Store the strongest supported category and preserve earlier steps in the extract. Do not infer causation from chronological association. A seller's claim that a message worked stays a seller explanation unless corroborated.

## Useful context

Record observed buyer language, problem, value claim, objection and alternative/status quo only when present. Do not add attractive but unsupported company attributes. Preserve company/product claims as claims made in the message, not verified proof of the product's performance.
