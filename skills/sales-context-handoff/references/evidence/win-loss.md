# Interpret wins and losses

Modified 2026-11-20 from zarif3624/analyze-win-loss; see [NOTICE.md](../../NOTICE.md).

Audit duplicates, original outcomes, missing fields, reopened deals and inconsistent dates before comparing anything. Preserve opportunity identity and selection reasons. Do not count the same opportunity twice because it appears in an export and CRM.

Keep buyer evidence, seller interpretation and recorded fields distinct. A CRM reason code is a recorded explanation, not the complete cause. Price mentioned in a lost deal does not prove price caused the loss.

Reconstruct only what is supported: desired change, alternatives including inaction, decision criteria, stakeholders, decisive events and final outcome. Multiple factors can contribute.

Compare fit explanations with observed handling: assignment, acceptance, response, follow-up and stage progression. A loss alongside delayed follow-up does not establish either poor fit or a follow-up cause. Distinguish evidence for fit, execution and timing; retain competing explanations when the records cannot separate them. Do not turn a loss reason or an unworked prospect into an ICP exclusion.

Revisit the business symptoms and explanations captured at setup. For each material concern, state what the sample supports, challenges or leaves unresolved, with cited cases and coverage limits. Selected cases can illustrate a mechanism but cannot establish a company-wide trend or its root cause without suitable broader evidence. Put the smallest useful check for unresolved explanations in gaps rather than prescribing a new motion or implementation.

Describe patterns with counts, denominator, evidence coverage, counterexamples and alternative explanations. Use "was reported as", "co-occurred with" or "may indicate" where causation is not established. Do not rank individual sellers or infer buyer personality.

Make comparisons as comparable as the available evidence permits. Consider differences in offer, source, segment, period, sales handling and time allowed to reach the outcome before interpreting a contrast. Prefer similar cases or groups with similar observation time when readily available; do not split a small sample into unsupported segment claims. Recent open opportunities are not losses, and a younger group with fewer conversions is not necessarily weaker. If dates or context are missing, retain descriptive observations and state the limitation rather than inventing a maturity cutoff or requiring more collection.

Keep population-level report figures distinct from the intentionally selected examples. Attribute reported rates with their known definitions, period and denominator; do not recompute a company win rate from sampled wins/losses or combine mismatched report populations. These are best-effort checks during interpretation, not a requirement to build a funnel report or conduct a statistical study.

Sparse evidence should yield a short set of observations and gaps, not a confident story. No new interviews, competitive research, roadmap promises, discounts or process rollout are part of this milestone.
