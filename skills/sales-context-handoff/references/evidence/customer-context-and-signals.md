# Customer context, signals and triggers

Collect this evidence from the selected records, conversations and supplied documents. Do not research missing attributes or start monitoring prospects. Include known prospects and lost cases as well as customers; for noncommercial work, use participant context rather than inventing purchasing needs.

## Capture during collection

Preserve relevant passages about the person's or organization's situation: goals, problems and consequences, current workflow/tools, alternatives or workarounds, prior attempts, constraints, decision criteria and urgency. Attribute buyer statements, seller explanations and recorded fields separately. A company descriptor or job title alone does not explain a need.

Look for dated events or changes that may explain why a conversation or decision happened then. Capture who linked the event to the decision and when. Keep the original event date distinct from when it was recorded or learned. Missing context stays unknown; do not infer it from a win or a reply. Link the context to the existing account/contact, opportunity or event instance, offer and outcome in the extracts.

## Customer-context report

Write `customer-context.md` around the situations found in the sample, using named, linked cases. Summarize needs, current state, desired change, alternatives, constraints and decision criteria with exact buyer language where useful. Include differences between positive, negative and unknown-outcome cases, not just an idealized customer story. Cite sources and state coverage. This file describes observed situations; `icp.md` synthesizes which situations may indicate fit, and `sales-context.md` describes the seller's process.

## Signals and triggers report

Write `signals-and-triggers.md` with these distinctions:

- A **signal** is an observable condition or behavior that may indicate fit, need, readiness or timing.
- A **trigger** is an event or change reported or observed to precede a need or decision. Sequence alone does not establish that it caused the sale.
- A **hypothesis** proposes how either might help identify similar future cases; it is not a demonstrated predictor.

For each useful candidate, give its precise description, named supporting cases and outcomes, event/observation timing, counterexamples and missing evidence. State whether it was externally observable before outreach, known only in private CRM/conversations, or learned after the outcome. If external availability is only proposed, label it unverified and describe what would need checking later. Do not turn a private buyer disclosure or post-sale fact into a ready-to-use prospecting signal.

Compare across sampled outcomes where possible and report counts with coverage. Note alternative explanations, signal-present negative cases and signal-absent positive cases when found. Keep confirmed buyer-reported triggers separate from seller theories and inferred patterns. Do not claim predictive accuracy, causation, new scoring weights or calibrated thresholds from this sample. Record what further evidence would test the candidate; no enrichment or numerical model is built here.

If no credible signals or triggers were captured, state that explicitly and identify the missing source coverage. Keep the report short instead of filling it with generic ideas.
